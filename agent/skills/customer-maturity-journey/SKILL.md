# Vercel Customer Maturity Journey

## Purpose

Use this skill to assess a customer’s ability to create, sustain, and extend measurable value from its digital experiences and relevant Vercel capabilities. Turi uses the assessment to provide evidence-based recommendations inside Turas; the maturity journey itself belongs to neither Turi nor Turas.

The journey is a customer-value model, not a Vercel sales stage, adoption score, contract tier, feature checklist, or a promise that every customer should use every Vercel product. It helps Vercel FDE/PS teams choose the right next outcome, delivery method, and level of support for the customer’s actual context.

Assess a defined scope — a customer journey, application, product area, business unit, or authorized portfolio — not an entire company by default. A customer can be highly mature in release collaboration and early in AI governance, or advanced in one region and constrained in another. Customers may also include personas beyond software engineers. Do not average unrelated capabilities into a misleading single label.

## Assessment Principles

- **Customer outcome first.** Maturity is the customer’s increasing ability to achieve desired outcomes safely and repeatedly. Vercel product adoption is evidence only when it has a demonstrated connection to that ability.
- **Evidence before labels.** State scope, assessment version, observation period, source, freshness, and confidence. Distinguish source facts, derived signals, and interpretation.
- **Non-linear by design.** Stages describe the primary next capability to develop, not a universal sequence. A customer may enter at any stage, progress dimensions at different rates, or intentionally remain at a stage because it fits its needs.
- **Build with the customer.** A maturity plan should strengthen customer ownership, skills, operating habits, partnership, and decision rights. It must not create artificial FDE/PS or platform dependency.
- **Prefer the smallest meaningful next move.** Recommend a bounded improvement to a priority journey or workflow, with a baseline and an accountable owner, rather than a broad “modernization” mandate.
- **Respect the estate.** Preserve the customer’s systems of record, identity, data, and specialized workloads where they fit. Position Vercel where it improves the customer-facing experience, delivery loop, security, observability, processes, or AI operating model.
- **Treat AI as a cross-cutting capability.** AI can be relevant at any stage, but production AI requires appropriate evaluation, data controls, human authority, observability, and operational ownership. A chatbot or model call does not establish AI maturity.
- **Do not infer maturity from account sentiment, revenue, plan type, FDE presence, or a color-coded score.** Those may be context; they are not evidence.

## The Maturity Journey

| Stage | Primary Capability | Supporting Evidence | FDE/PS Focus | Do not mistake it for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **1. Explore** | The customer can identify a consequential digital/customer workflow, an accountable owner, and a credible value hypothesis. | Sponsor and working team; current-state workflow; baseline or discoverable measurement; constraints and decision path understood. | Outcome discovery, architecture/context assessment, baseline, scoped proof plan, and engagement qualification. | A generic technology interest, an executive mandate without an owner, or a product demo. |
| **2. Activate** | The customer can deliver a bounded, production-shaped improvement with a named operating owner. | First scope and acceptance criteria; secure/reviewable delivery path; production/readiness evidence appropriate to risk; handoff plan. | Establish the first trustworthy slice, strengthen customer collaboration, and prove a measurable outcome. | A prototype, a migration announcement, or an environment created without operating ownership. |
| **3. Accelerate** | Cross-functional teams can ship and learn repeatedly with reduced release/review friction. | Repeatable delivery workflow; shared review process; defined release controls; cycle-time/quality evidence; non-engineering stakeholder participation where relevant. | Codify the working delivery play; improve review, feedback, content/product collaboration, and release confidence. | More deployments alone, or developer speed achieved by bypassing controls and business feedback. |
| **4. Optimize** | The customer measures and improves the real end-user experience, reliability, security, and cost/performance trade-offs for priority journeys. | Real user/performance and operational evidence; defined ownership; reliability/security controls; post-release learning; outcome comparison to baseline. | Focus on the most valuable customer journeys, observable quality, resilient operations, and data-backed optimization. | A synthetic benchmark, an isolated Lighthouse score, or a single incident-free release. |
| **5. Scale** | The customer can extend proven practices across teams, regions, applications, or brands without losing governance, context, or quality. | Shared standards and reusable patterns; appropriate access/governance model; multi-team operating cadence; capacity/ownership model; comparable metrics. | Platform enablement, reusable architectures, regional/organizational governance, and support for the teams that will operate independently. | Centralizing every decision, duplicating bespoke solutions, or declaring scale from one successful application. |
| **6. Transform** | The customer deliberately uses its digital and AI capabilities to create new customer or operating-model value, while continuously adapting with evidence. | Sustained outcome portfolio; intentional innovation governance; safe experimentation-to-production path; reusable learning; customer-owned operating model. | Co-innovate on strategically differentiated journeys, production AI where appropriate, and the next product/platform patterns. | Technology novelty, autonomous actions without controls, or an open-ended innovation lab. |

### How to Interpret Stages

- **Explore is a valid stage.** A customer that has not yet selected an owned, measurable problem should receive discovery support, not a prescriptive architecture or migration plan.
- **Activate is the first production-value threshold.** The customer has a bounded first slice and a credible ownership path; it is not necessarily broadly standardized.
- **Accelerate and Optimize often overlap.** Teams may improve collaboration/release cadence while simultaneously improving real user experience and operational confidence.
- **Scale is selective.** The right outcome may be one shared pattern or a regional operating model, not enterprise-wide standardization.
- **Transform is continual, not a finish line.** The customer remains accountable for operating, measuring, and evolving the results it creates.

## Maturity Dimensions

Use the stages to tell a coherent customer story, but assess the following dimensions independently. A material assessment should show each dimension’s observed state, evidence, confidence, and next improvement.

| Dimension | What to Assess | Examples of Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Outcome and Ownership** | Whether a customer objective, sponsor, operating owner, baseline, and decision rights are clear. | Approved outcome hypothesis; business/technical owner; baseline source; decision log; outcome review. |
| **Delivery and Collaboration** | Ability to build, review, test, release, and learn across engineering, product, design, content, marketing, security, and operations as needed. | Protected preview/review workflow; release cadence; PR/deployment evidence; stakeholder feedback; release/rollback process. |
| **Experience and Adoption** | Whether priority users receive a useful, accessible, performant experience and actually adopt the intended workflow. | Real user performance; usability/adoption evidence; customer journey metrics; support/feedback signals; conversion/engagement measure where relevant. |
| **Operational Trust** | Security, privacy, identity, reliability, observability, incident/change handling, data controls, and support ownership appropriate to the workload. | Access model; security review; observability/alerts; runbook; incident/recovery evidence; residency/retention controls. |
| **Platform and Organization** | Ability to reuse standards, manage multi-team/region complexity, govern access and spend, and develop customer capability without bottlenecks. | Shared patterns; documentation; code/repository ownership; role/access governance; enablement; common metrics; regional operating rules. |
| **Innovation and AI** | Ability to select valuable experiments and safely turn validated opportunities into production workflows. | Experiment hypothesis; evaluation/acceptance criteria; model/data/tool controls; human authority; outcome evidence; product-learning disposition. |

Use a qualitative state for each dimension rather than a fabricated precise score:

| State | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Unknown** | Evidence is missing, stale, contradictory, or outside the requester’s scope. |
| **Emerging** | Important work is ad hoc or person-dependent; the customer has not yet demonstrated repeatability. |
| **Established** | A bounded practice exists for the assessed scope and has an accountable owner. |
| **Measured** | The customer uses evidence to improve the practice and can explain important trade-offs. |
| **Scaled** | The practice is reusable and governed across the relevant teams/regions without erasing necessary local context. |
| **Adaptive** | The customer continuously experiments, learns, and improves while preserving operating controls and ownership. |

Do not compute an overall maturity score by averaging these states. The recommended maturity milestone is the smallest customer-valued capability that addresses the primary constraint and can be supported by available evidence.

## Assessment Workflow

### 1. Define the assessment boundary

Confirm the account, customer journey/application/business unit, geography, intended audience, decision to support, and observation period. State the maturity-model version.

Examples of valid boundaries:

- “The checkout experience in North America, before peak season.”
- “The content publishing workflow for the global marketing organization.”
- “The internal AI agent used by the support organization.”
- “The first two product teams adopting a shared release workflow.”

Avoid “the customer is Stage 4” without a boundary; it hides meaningful differences and often turns a useful assessment into account labeling.

### 2. Gather minimum authorized evidence

Retrieve the smallest authorized evidence set that can support the requested decision:

- account and stakeholder records, including customer objectives and named owners;
- engagement/delivery state, decision log, scope, risks, and handoff evidence;
- customer-approved architecture, identity, data, regional, security, and support constraints;
- deployment/review, performance, reliability, security, adoption, or workflow-outcome evidence relevant to the boundary;
- maturity assessments and metric definitions already recorded for the same scope.

Surface stale data, conflicts, and missing sources prominently. Ask for or propose the smallest discovery action needed; never fill a gap with a generic maturity claim.

### 3. Establish the current profile

For each relevant dimension, state:

1. observed state;
2. evidence and freshness;
3. confidence and important unknowns;
4. customer consequence; and
5. the constraint that most limits the desired outcome.

Then name the current journey stage as a concise narrative, for example: “The customer is activating a first production workflow: outcome ownership and delivery collaboration are established for this scope, while operational trust and adoption evidence remain incomplete.”

### 4. Select the next maturity milestone

Recommend one primary next milestone and, if helpful, one supporting milestone. A strong milestone has:

- a customer-valued outcome rather than a feature installation;
- a named customer sponsor and operating owner;
- a baseline, success measure, and observation window;
- a bounded scope and explicit non-goals;
- an appropriate delivery engagement/phase from the `delivery-methodology` skill;
- a safe path for security, support, adoption, and ownership; and
- a clear stop, handoff, or expand decision.

Load `delivery-methodology` before recommending a material engagement, delivery plan, statement of work, stage gate, handoff, or capacity model. The maturity journey selects the customer capability to develop; the delivery methodology governs how Vercel FDE/PS teams deliver it.

### 5. Connect the milestone to an appropriate Vercel role

Explain the Vercel capability only after the customer constraint and outcome are clear. Verify current feature availability, plan/contract fit, limits, data handling, and regional suitability against current Vercel documentation before making a commitment.

| Maturity Emphasis | Vercel Capability Examples (when relevant) | Customer Value to Test |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Explore / Activate | Next.js/Vercel delivery foundation, controlled Preview Deployments, Deployment Protection, an incremental migration or first production slice. For an internal app or agent, Vercel Passport can make the first usable experience private behind the customer’s identity provider. | Can the team deliver and validate a valuable change with the right people involved and an owned path to production? |
| Accelerate | Preview/review collaboration, Git-based delivery, protected environments, reusable release practices, and appropriate developer/content/product feedback loops. | Has review-to-release friction decreased without weakening quality, security, or stakeholder involvement? |
| Optimize | Speed Insights, Observability, caching/runtime improvements, Vercel Firewall, and production check/release practices. | Are real users receiving a measurably faster, more reliable, safer experience on the prioritized journey? |
| Scale | Enterprise governance features where appropriate (for example, RBAC, SSO/Directory Sync, access controls, audit/observability), Conformance, shared patterns, Vercel’s Security Dashboard/BYOC where qualified, and multi-team operational practices. | Can additional teams/regions use proven patterns without creating a central bottleneck or eroding controls? |
| Transform | AI Gateway, Sandbox, Workflows/Queues, Vercel Connect, governed agents, and AI evaluation/approval patterns where they fit a high-value workflow. Vercel Agent may be considered for platform operations only when its permissions, review, and operating model are appropriate. | Can the customer turn a validated AI/digital capability into a safe, measurable, customer-owned operating advantage? |

Never recommend a migration, enterprise plan, AI feature, or additional Vercel product as the maturity milestone itself. The milestone is the customer ability/outcome; platform capability is a means to it.

## Ship 2026 agentic capability arc

The Ship 2026 releases expand the possible maturity journey; they do **not** replace it with a product-adoption ladder. Use this arc only where an agentic or full-stack opportunity is tied to a customer outcome. A customer may enter at any point, and may choose not to pursue an agentic path at all.

| Potential Capability Step | Likely Maturity Emphasis | Customer Capability to Establish | Relevant Ship Capability (when qualified) | Minimum Evidence (before moving on) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **1. Establish a safe starting boundary** | Explore / Activate | A named workflow, data/identity owner, user group, risk boundary, and first production-shaped slice. The first use case can be an internal app, an assistive agent, or a service modernization—not autonomous action. | Vercel Passport for private-by-default internal access; Dockerfile support, Container Registry, and Vercel Services where an existing backend/service is the practical path; Preview Deployments and Deployment Protection for controlled validation. | Sponsor and operating owner; approved data/access boundary; a bounded outcome measure; explicit human authority and stop conditions. |
| **2. Make one task useful and governed** | Activate / Accelerate | An agent or workflow can reach the right enterprise systems under an explicit application or user identity, with authorization limited to the task, and people can review its output or proposed action. | Vercel Connect as the delegated external-access layer: reusable connectors, project/environment controls, user/app identity, and fine-grained runtime authorization. AI SDK 7/AI Gateway for provider-flexible model use; Chat SDK when the approved interaction surface is Slack, GitHub, or another supported channel. | Permission map; source-of-truth/data-quality evidence; evaluation cases; approval/escalation path; evidence that the workflow improves a real user or operator outcome. |
| **3. Prove production reliability and bounded autonomy** | Optimize | Multi-step work is durable, observable, recoverable, and limited by reversibility and blast radius. Autonomy grows only as the customer can observe and govern it. | Workflow SDK, Queues, Sandbox, Observability, and AI Gateway; Vercel Agent for reviewable platform investigation/fix proposals when its beta status and permission model fit. | Evaluation and failure-mode results; trace/alert ownership; cost/latency and quality baselines; rollback/containment design; measured benefit over the non-agent path. |
| **4. Enable many teams without losing control** | Scale | Reusable agent/service patterns, shared guardrails, delegated ownership, and a consistent security posture across teams and projects. | Security Dashboard and BYOC on AWS only where availability, contract, and architecture qualify; Conformance, identity/access controls, Connect/Passport patterns, shared service and deployment conventions. | Reusable reference pattern; exception process; team enablement and support model; comparable quality/security evidence; no central delivery bottleneck. |
| **5. Redesign the experience or operating model** | Transform | The customer safely uses validated capabilities to change how work is done or how users reach value—for example, intent-led discovery, internal decision support, or agent-assisted operations. | The combined Agent Stack and full-stack platform, selected only for the proven workflow; an agentic interface may complement rather than replace human-led experience. | Sustained customer or operating outcome; customer-owned governance and economics; periodic evaluation; explicit decisions about where a human remains essential. |

### Use the arc to choose a next move, not a narrative

- Treat **Connect** as the delegated access and identity-control plane between an agent/service and an external system: it supports reusable connectors, project/environment boundaries, app/user/federated subjects, scoped provider authorization, and verified triggers. Avoid calling it a secrets-management product. Removing long-lived provider secrets from the application runtime is an important implementation benefit of its credential exchange model, not its core category.
- Treat **Passport and permissions** as readiness and governance enablers, not proof that an agent is ready for production.
- Treat **Vercel Agent** as a separate platform-operations opportunity: it can progress from read-only investigation to reviewable proposed fixes, but it must not be conflated with the customer building a customer-facing agent.
- Treat **Vercel Services and container support** as an incremental full-stack modernization option. They may help a customer bring an existing backend closer to its frontend and previews; they do not require a microservices rewrite.
- Evaluate the agent experience for non-developer users—operators, content teams, service teams, analysts, and end customers—alongside developer experience. The customer value, ownership, and controls remain the gate.
- Before recommending a Ship release, verify its current GA/beta status, plan, regional availability, data handling, and contract fit in official documentation. Do not imply availability from this framework.

## Maturity-informed delivery plays

Use the delivery-methodology lifecycle for execution. The maturity model changes the emphasis and minimum evidence—not the approval, security, or customer-ownership standards.

| Journey Stage | Typical Delivery Play | Expected Exit Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Explore | Outcome discovery, architecture/maturity assessment, baseline, and scoped proof charter. | Sponsor, working team, measurable outcome hypothesis, constraints, and accept/reshape/defer/decline decision. |
| Activate | Production-shaped vertical slice, incremental modernization, first secure delivery/review path, and named handoff owner. | Bounded outcome evidence; operating owner; production/readiness/handoff plan appropriate to risk. |
| Accelerate | Delivery workflow and collaboration improvement; repeatable playbook; release and preview governance; enablement. | Measured reduction in relevant friction, stronger quality/review evidence, and customer-owned repeatable practice. |
| Optimize | Priority-journey performance/reliability/security improvement with real-user and operational measurement. | Baseline comparison, observable improvement, accountable operating owner, and post-release learning. |
| Scale | Multi-team/region pattern rollout, governance/enablement, shared metric definitions, and supported reusable architectures. | Teams can independently apply the pattern within defined guardrails; comparable evidence and exceptions are visible. |
| Transform | Strategic co-innovation on a differentiated digital or AI workflow, with a production and customer-ownership path from day one. | Measurable workflow value, evaluation/control evidence where relevant, customer operating ownership, and a deliberate product/field-learning disposition. |

## Portfolio use

Use the maturity journey to prioritize conversation and investment, not to rank customers or people. A portfolio view may group authorized scopes by their primary next milestone, confidence, risk, value potential, and required FDE/PS capability.

Useful portfolio questions:

- Which accounts have a validated outcome but lack an operating owner or production path?
- Where is delivery collaboration the binding constraint rather than architecture?
- Which priority journeys have real-user performance/reliability evidence that warrants optimization?
- Which patterns have enough repeatability and customer ownership to standardize or enable across teams?
- Where does a strategic AI opportunity lack the required governance, evaluation, or human-authority evidence?

Do not use maturity labels to predict renewal, account health, spend, individual performance, or commercial potential without separate authorized evidence.

## Turi behavior while using this skill

1. Load this skill before assessing maturity, comparing an authorized maturity portfolio, or drafting a maturity-informed plan. Load `delivery-methodology` as well when the request includes engagement planning, delivery stages, handoff, capacity, or scaling.
2. Confirm the assessment boundary and retrieve authorized source data. Cite source, time, model/metric version, confidence, and gaps.
3. Report a dimension profile and primary next milestone; do not present an unsupported single score or universal customer label.
4. Clearly separate source fact, derived signal, and recommendation. State the customer outcome, trade-offs, owner, measure, and decision needed.
5. Treat recommendations and maturity plans as drafts. Creating an assessment record, changing a lifecycle stage, starting a playbook, assigning staffing, sending a customer communication, or modifying a customer/system record requires an explicit approval-gated tool/workflow.
6. If evidence is missing or access is unavailable, explain the limitation and propose a focused discovery step; do not generate a maturity stage from generic industry assumptions.

## Required Turas records and deterministic rules

Turas should store the maturity journey as structured, authorized data; the skill guides interpretation but must not be the only place the model/scoring logic exists.

At a minimum, maintain:

- assessment scope, assessment date, assessor, model version, observation window, and confidence;
- dimension states, source evidence, freshness, missing inputs, and exceptions;
- current-stage narrative, primary constraint, next milestone, owner, success measure, and review date;
- link to associated engagement/delivery stage, decision, risk, outcome baseline, and handoff evidence;
- Vercel capability considered, customer requirement it addresses, alternatives, decision rationale, and any plan/region/contract qualification;
- assessment history and approved changes as append-only records alongside the current profile.

Implement deterministic state mappings and any score calculations in versioned application/domain modules and metric definitions. Turi may explain the result but must not silently alter a stage, recalculate with undisclosed criteria, or treat a prompt as an audit record.
